Ayiti Nouvo Kontra SosyalSovereign Fortress

A design for Haiti · 47 parts · revised 21 Oct 2026

Citizens chosen by lottery. Experts who draft. Courts that check everyone. Every payment public.

This page turns the full 47-part document into something you can click through. It keeps the two goals you set: stop corruption and capture, and build national wealth while taking part in world trade without being at the mercy of it.

It is a proposal, not a governing text. Its honest route is to be adopted by candidates and civil society inside the real 2026–27 election cycle, then written into a constitution by an elected body.

The four pillars

No branch can detain, fine, seize assets or silence the press without a court that answers to none of the other three. Tap a pillar.

How a law is made

1. MandateThe Citizens’ Assembly sets what the country needs.
2. DraftExpert civil servants investigate and write the text.
3. Pre-checkThe Constitutional Court checks it against entrenched rules.
4. Read-backExperts present it in plain spoken Kreyòl.
5. VoteThe Assembly votes. The draft and every change is public.

The same sequence runs the annual budget. Every budget line is posted on the public ledger before the vote.

What the recheck found

Facts that moved

The election is now 13 December 2026. Haiti now counts 149 communes. The Gang Suppression Force is authorised at 5,500 and still under 1,000 deployed. The US put a 25% duty on Brazil in July 2026, and its case named Pix.

Design fixes

The Judicial Ethics Commission contradiction is resolved. The military limit is now entrenched. Seat allocation became an explicit choice. The health claim that said “no risk” was corrected.

Still yours to decide

Twenty open decisions remain. The biggest one: the document has no cost model yet.

Who decides

Power is spread across people chosen in different ways and on different clocks. Nobody can be bought once and win.

The Citizens’ Assembly: 150 seats

Drawn by lottery from every adult citizen. Two-year term, no second term. No degree needed. Everyone drawn gets paid training in spoken Kreyòl before their first vote. Pick a seat rule and watch how it treats each département.

Share of seatsShare of population

Population: 2015 official estimates (IHSI, via Wikipedia). Seats by largest-remainder rounding.

Protecting the smaller départements

Two safeguards are proposed, and the choice is yours.

Members vote as people

A seat drawn in Ouest is not an instruction to vote for Ouest. There are no parties to build blocs. Regional conflict is still real on money and projects, so the next safeguard targets that.

Proposed

Double majority for sharing money

A law that divides money or projects between départements needs a majority of all seats and also a majority of the seats within at least 6 of the 10 départements. The biggest département cannot decide alone who gets roads, water and clinics.

Proposed

Départements count in referendums

A constitutional change needs a national majority and a majority in at least 6 of 10 départements, as Switzerland requires a majority of cantons.

Executive Council: 7

Elected by the Assembly. Six-month terms. The ceremonial president rotates every 26 days (7 × 26 = 182 days). It coordinates and does not make law. In a sudden disaster it can act for 48 hours, then the Assembly ratifies or reverses.

Added in recheck No one serves a second term until four years after the last one, and no one serves more than two terms in a lifetime.

Five Diaspora Tribunes

One per continent. They speak in the Assembly and do not vote. Their records are public. A Tribune under pressure from a host country can ask for reassignment with no penalty. This protection is partial, and the document says so.

Communal Assemblies

Each commune has its own lottery assembly. Commune Officers and Section Agents (tiers 6 and 7) answer to it, not to a party boss.

The lottery is open code

The draw software is published in full. The first draw runs under civil-society and international observation so people can trust it before it relies on code review alone.

Which vote does this need?

Choose an action. Numbers are out of 150 Assembly seats.

Removing someone who turns corrupt

Assembly member

DSI refers the case. The Administrative Court makes a finding. A lottery-drawn panel of other members decides, never the full body voting on its own colleague. A new draw fills the seat, never the “next name on a list.”

Council member

DSI referral, a judicial finding, then a simple Assembly majority. The Assembly elects the replacement.

Judge

A finding from the cross-selected Judicial Ethics Commission plus a two-thirds Assembly vote.

The civil service and eight ministries

The working state is a career of exam-selected experts. The eight ministries merge Haiti’s existing ministries, so the change is how people get jobs, not a new map.

Who can join: the NASA exam

Open to any adult citizen

No degree is needed to sit the first stage (logic, maths, civics). Free preparation courses run at the Sovereign House in each commune, so preparing doesn’t take money or a trip to Port-au-Prince.

Generalist track

Pass, start paid service, and learn through state-funded part-time study. For Section Agents and entry Commune Officers.

Professional track

Pass the aptitude exam, get fully funded medical, engineering or law education, then serve a fixed term in return. For doctors, engineers, judges and economists.

Blind grading, audited

Exams are graded anonymously. An independent panel re-grades random samples and flags odd patterns, and results are published. This guards against the lesson from France’s ENA and India’s UPSC, where “merit” exams ended up filtering for wealth.

Two bodies that had no home

Proposed in recheck

DSI (anti-corruption)

Standalone directorate outside every ministry, with its own budget line. Its director is chosen like the Central Bank Governor: cross-selected nomination, Assembly supermajority, one long term. It can only investigate and refer. Courts issue every warrant, freeze and detention. Its own investigators face the same lifestyle audits they run on others.

Proposed in recheck

Cyber-Defense Directorate

Sits in the Foreign Affairs and Sovereign Defense ministry. Defence and ledger stress-testing are routine. Any offensive action needs Executive Council authorisation first and judicial review within 30 days.

Who checks the checkers

One test for every office: if this were captured or abused, what stops it, and is that a different body, chosen a different way, on a different clock?

Random case assignment

Every case is assigned by a logged random draw. Wealthy litigants can’t shop for a judge.

Public Defender Service

Paid at the same grade as prosecutors, so the free option is excellent. Anyone can still hire private counsel.

Guaranteed budget

A constitutional minimum share of the national budget goes to the courts. No branch can starve them into obedience.

Changing the rules of the rules

Ordinary

Two-thirds plus referendum

Ministry structure, tariff rates, administrative detail. Citizens can force a proposal onto the ballot with signatures from about 5% of registered voters (the Swiss model).

Entrenched

Three-quarters, unanimous Council, referendum with minimum turnout

  • An independent judiciary
  • Sortition as the way the Assembly is chosen
  • No guilt by association
  • Press freedom, no prior restraint
  • Open, uncredentialed eligibility for the lottery pool
  • Added in recheck No independent domestic policing powers for the military

The Constitutional Court reviews an amendment only to confirm it doesn’t sneak in an entrenched change. It is not a general veto over policy it dislikes.

Gaps no rule can close

The founding moment

A Constituent Assembly has no earlier constitution to test it, which is true of every founding in history. Broad membership, outside observers and a supermajority reduce the risk and cannot remove it.

Gang-held territory

A Communal Assembly under gang control can be intimidated in ways no org chart reaches. Rollout follows security, commune by commune.

Diaspora Tribunes abroad

A host government holds their visas. Transparency and reassignment rights reduce the exposure. They don’t end it.

Money, trade and land

Corruption happens at award, not just at payment. So the ledger shows bids before awards, and the payment system is owned by the country using it.

The public ledger

A live copy sits at every commune’s Sovereign House. A change counts only if over 70% of copies match, so editing one record in Port-au-Prince does nothing. Updated The earlier text said 140 nodes. With 149 communes, it is one node per commune.

  • Logged at the moment of action: taxes, every budget line, contracts and payment stages, all civil-servant salaries and asset disclosures (tiers 1–4), court rulings and case draws, farmer purchase prices, Assembly votes with a visible diff on every bill, diaspora bond spending.
  • Narrow carve-outs only: individual health records, pre-charge investigations, minors’ identities, witness protection, and operational details of border deployments (never their budgets).
  • Air-gapped treasury: reserve systems can push signed records out. Nothing outside can reach in.
  • Signed by the person: each entry carries the individual official’s signature, not a department login.
  • Three ways to read it: web, SMS/USSD on a basic phone, and a staffed terminal at every Sovereign House, in spoken and written Kreyòl.
  • Open source: both the ledger and the lottery code are published.
How contracts are awarded
  • Contracts above a threshold go to open tender, published before the award, so the public sees who is competing.
  • A firm that a court finds paid or took a bribe is barred for 10 years, permanently for a repeat. The list is public.
  • A set share of procurement is reserved for Haitian-owned small firms and farmer cooperatives.
Budget cycle and surviving a two-year Assembly turnover
  • Fixed fiscal calendar that no branch can stall. Ministries request funds against Assembly priorities. The Administrative Court can flag an unrealistic revenue forecast before the vote.
  • Ministers (5 years), Directors-General (permanent) and judges (12 years) outlast any Assembly.
  • Capital projects pass by two-thirds, and cancelling one takes two-thirds as well.
  • A rolling 5–10 year National Development Plan that each Assembly amends and doesn’t rewrite.
  • The outgoing Assembly’s last session includes a handover listing every active project and contract.
National payment rail (RPN)

The independent central bank runs a shared instant-settlement pipe, Rezo Peman Nasyonal. Banks, credit unions (kès popilè) and mobile-money firms connect and keep their own apps. This copies how Brazil’s Pix actually works. It is not one state app.

  • Reached by app, by SMS/USSD, and by an agent terminal at each Sovereign House. Send money to a phone number or ID.
  • Carries civil-service pay, taxes, farmer payments, pensions, health premiums and the last leg of remittances.
  • Domestic only. It settles gourdes between Haitian accounts. It is not sold as a replacement for SWIFT or correspondent banks.

Why the caution holds. On 22 July 2026 the US put an extra 25% duty on most Brazilian imports. The investigation behind it listed Pix first, citing policies that “favor Brazil’s Pix system over U.S. electronic payment service providers.” Framing the RPN as financial inclusion infrastructure is a deliberate safeguard.

Staying connected to the world’s banks

The bigger threat than a SWIFT cutoff is quiet “de-risking”: global banks drop small Caribbean clients because compliance costs too much.

  • Never depend on one correspondent bank or one country.
  • The public ledger lowers perceived laundering risk, so banks find Haiti cheaper to keep.
  • Join regional settlement efforts rather than build a go-it-alone system. Softened The draft credited CARICOM by name for a specific settlement strategy. That attribution wasn’t re-verified, so it now says “regional efforts.”
  • Watch stablecoin and multi-bank digital-currency pilots. Don’t build on them yet.

Honest limit: even a clean Haiti pays some cost for regional risk perception that no single country controls.

Trade: reduce exposure, don’t chase self-sufficiency
  • Public export-concentration index. Target: no single country over 40% of any export category within 10 years.
  • Use several remittance channels, since remittances are around a fifth of GDP.
  • State stockpile of rice and fuel measured in months.
  • Deeper CARICOM trade. Use the bloc as a shield.
  • Customs arms-interdiction unit at the four border posts (Ouanaminthe, Belladère, Malpasse, Anse-à-Pitres), gun tracing with source countries, and seizure data on the ledger.

Open check The standing 50% tariff on Dominican goods may clash with WTO non-discrimination rules and the CARICOM–DR trade agreement. A trade lawyer needs to review it before anyone relies on it.

Loans, aid and the “never concede” rule
  • Loans with policy conditions: two-thirds Assembly plus a referendum, not an absolute ban. A rigid “never” breaks in a real emergency.
  • No parallel governments. No foreign organisation runs independent operations in Haiti. Aid arrives as cash through the treasury, logged on the ledger.
  • Emergency relief in an acute disaster can be accepted under the 48-hour authority, then ratified or rejected by the Assembly.
  • Nobody can quietly concede. Anything touching sovereignty, trade terms, resources or territory needs two-thirds ratification. Courtesy is free. Authority to cave doesn’t exist.
  • First-call lenders: Caribbean Development Bank, Inter-American Development Bank, South-South partners.
Land: the cadastre comes first

Nearly everything economic depends on knowing who owns what. A new Land Cadastre directorate (inside the Economy ministry) maps commune by commune, using satellite and GPS plus community adjudication, as Rwanda did.

  • Long-term customary occupancy counts as a valid claim, so the poor aren’t dispossessed for lacking paper that never existed.
  • Disputes go to the local Civic Tribunal, with appeal to the Court of First Instance. The registry lives on the public ledger, which blocks backdated or double-sold deeds.
Pensions: a floor for everyone
  • Universal floor paid from mining royalties and export profits, modelled on Bolivia’s Renta Dignidad. It reaches farmers and informal workers.
  • Top-up for formal workers, paid by worker and employer, set in the yearly three-party talks.
  • Run by a new Retirement and Social Security directorate in the Health and Social Dignity ministry.

Health, homes, children and food

These are the parts people feel every day. Each one is built to run on the same commune network, with real timelines.

Health insurance copied from Japan

Everyone is enrolled

No opt-out and no form to file. Salaried workers pay a share of pay, split with the employer. Farmers and informal workers pay through their commune by income tier. Below a set income the premium is zero.

One national price list

A Health Fee Schedule Commission (government, doctors, civil-service economists) sets one price per procedure and drug, revised every two years. Every hospital charges the same.

Monthly out-of-pocket cap

Past a limit set by income tier, the state pays the rest. It triggers automatically on the ledger. Illness can’t bankrupt a family.

Any provider, same cover

The same benefits everywhere, so rural patients reach the same hospitals on the same terms.

No for-profit hospitals in the system

State or registered non-profit owners only. Private clinics may exist but cannot bill the national insurance.

Lower co-pays for kids and elders

Plus one flagship specialist hospital per département, funded by multi-year appropriations and diaspora bonds. Doctors’ training is funded in exchange for a service term.

Corrected The earlier draft said this carries no external risk. That overclaimed. It is far lower profile than a payment rail, but a national price list squeezes drug, device and insurance margins, and a non-profit-only rule affects foreign hospital operators. Expect pushback on medicine prices. Publish how drugs are bought and don’t pretend it is risk-free.

Housing: Vienna’s mechanism, not Vienna’s budget

Vienna’s insight is a large permanent stock of non-profit homes with cost-based rents that caps what the whole market can charge. Step through the order Haiti has to follow.

Honest scale: Vienna took about a century to reach roughly 40% non-speculative housing from a far richer base. Years 1–3 here are upgrading and emergency housing. The first cooperative units come in years 3–5, and only where the cadastre has cleared title.

Child protection
  1. Trace and reunite with family first.
  2. Kinship or foster care second.
  3. Small community group care third.
  4. Large institutions only as a court-ordered last resort.
  • Compulsory school enrollment doubles as detection. A child kept out of school is the clearest sign of a restavek household.
  • Street-outreach teams work through the Section Agent network, with a confidential reporting line at each Sovereign House.
  • Every residential home needs a licence, unannounced inspections published on the ledger, background-vetted staff, a whistleblower channel that bypasses management, and a ban on casual visitors (“orphanage tourism”).
  • A victim-centered anti-trafficking unit in the prosecution service. Victims are never prosecuted for offences tied to their exploitation. Transplant hospitals must verify organ sourcing or lose their licence.
  • Consent and dignity rules for children’s images in fundraising. All child charity money routes through the state channel.
  • Everyone who works with children is vetted and has a legal duty to report abuse.
Every child registered, and the naming rule
  • Every birth has a trained attendant: hospital, clinic or certified midwife (sage-femme).
  • The certificate is issued at delivery and logged in the civil registry.
  • Parents have 1–3 months to declare the child at city hall. Families facing real access barriers get a penalty-free extension, so the deadline never creates an undocumented child.
  • This stops the gap that leaves some deportees from the DR without papers.

Naming, as you decided: the registrar asks one question only, whether the name could reasonably expose the child to ridicule or harm (an obscenity, a slur, a number). Cultural or foreign origin is not checked. The family can appeal the registrar’s decision to the Civic Tribunal.

  • A state seed bank shields farmers from foreign seed patents and price swings.
  • Land title is a precondition. Farmers won’t invest in land they might lose.
  • The State Export Board (SEB) buys from farmers at a guaranteed price and pays instantly through the RPN.
Citizenship and the diaspora
  • Birth in Haiti to at least one Haitian parent, or descent from one, gives citizenship.
  • Dual citizenship is guaranteed for ordinary citizens. The 2012 amendment was applied unevenly, and the diaspora strategy can’t stand on an uncertain status.
  • One narrow exception: diplomats face a four-year limit afterwards on foreign residency or second citizenship, as an anti-blackmail rule.
  • Naturalization: five years of legal residence, Kreyòl proficiency, a clean record.
Families: complicity, not guilt by association
  • Helping a crime (hiding assets, forging papers, a false alibi) is ordinary complicity, tried individually with full defence rights.
  • Knowing and staying silent is not a crime for close family. Forcing relatives to inform on each other is how surveillance states work.
  • Instead: DSI lifestyle audits, plus whistleblower immunity and a share of recovered assets for anyone who comes forward.
  • Dynasty risk is handled by a four-year conflict-of-interest recusal. A close relative of an official can’t take a role or contract that conflicts with that office.
Press, internet and data rights
  • An independent commission (lottery citizens, press associations, law faculty) guards the rules. Nobody writes press law about themselves.
  • No prior restraint. Defamation is a civil matter in ordinary courts. Source protection. Media-ownership caps.
  • Net neutrality. A shutdown needs a judicial emergency finding plus Assembly ratification, with an automatic sunset.
  • A civil-society fact-checking body that publishes counter-information and has no power to remove anything. Media literacy in schools, following Finland.

Children first: orphanages, foreign abusers and honest fundraising

About 750 orphanages hold roughly 30,000 children, and the government estimates that about 80% of those children have a living parent. Money paid per child makes each child a source of income. Only 35 of 754 facilities inspected in 2018 met minimum standards. Two US founders of Haitian children’s homes have been convicted in US federal court of child sex crimes committed in Haiti.

A child is never a source of income

Poverty is never a reason to separate a child from a family. The order is: keep the child with the family, help the family, kinship and foster care, small community homes, and large residential care only as a last resort ordered by a court.

One register, one licence

Every child in residential care is recorded in a protected National Child Register. Every facility is licensed yearly and inspected without warning by the child-protection agency, the Protector of Citizens and two citizens drawn by lot.

Funding follows outcomes, not beds

Foreign money goes into a registered Haitian account posted on the ledger and is never paid per child. A foreign-founded home must be a Haitian entity with a Haitian-majority board, and its foreign sponsor is jointly liable for abuse.

Child-Safe Register

Every adult near children in residential care, Haitian or foreign, clergy or lay, is vetted with police clearance from every country they lived in, trained, and approved. Two adults at all times, no private rooms, no overnight stays. Removal is reported to their home country within 48 hours.

No orphanage tourism

Short visitors and mission groups may not care for, host or photograph children in homes. Volunteers are welcome in schools, clinics, building and skills work. Faith-based charities are welcome on the same terms.

Close the worst first, safely

Register everyone, trace families, and close the worst homes first, with a signed placement plan for each child before any closure. Pay families a fraction of what a bed costs so they can keep their children.

Abusers

Tried in Haitian courts whatever their passport. Haiti prepares a cooperation file for foreign prosecution, and a Victim Justice Fund pays for travel, protection, counselling and compensation so a Haitian child can reach justice abroad.

The Children’s Dignity Code

It regulates how organisations ask for money using children. No fundraising images of children in residential care, state custody or crisis distress without informed consent and a dignity review. Honest appeals that say how much of each gift reaches the child. Registered fundraisers with audited accounts. The child can later withdraw their image. It applies equally to foreign and Haitian groups, charities, churches and companies.

What it does not do. The state does not review, license, filter or take down reporting, opinion, photography of public events, or criticism of Haiti or its government. That would be a tool for hiding corruption in the hands of whoever governs next, and press freedom is entrenched. The better answer to a false or demeaning story is a better one: fund Haitian storytellers, and publish the facts.

A technology hub where young Haitians build and launch

A place where young Haitians, in Haiti and abroad, can build products and found companies without a connected family, a foreign sponsor or a bribe.

One campus, ten labs

A flagship campus in a city chosen by published criteria and public hearings, not by a minister, and ten small labs in the Sovereign Houses so a student in Fort-Liberté or Jérémie has the same tools on a screen.

What it offers

Solar power and redundant internet, company registration in one day, small grants paid in milestones, a co-investment fund that only invests alongside private investors (the Israeli Yozma model), mentors, and a safe test version of the national payment rail.

Fair entry

Blind review with no names or photos, a panel that includes citizens drawn by lot, ages 18 to 35, a second-chance track for graduates of the skills prisons and reintegration program, and published results.

Anti-capture rules

No equity for officials or their families. Founders keep their intellectual property. Foreign money is welcome but cannot carry control, data access or a board seat. Citizen data stays in Haiti. The board is drawn by lot.

Open Whether to require Haitian majority ownership for subsidised companies protects Haitian ownership but can deter investors. The hub city is also undecided.

Security and the outside world

Every armed body reports through one of two clear chains, Justice and Public Security or Foreign Affairs and Sovereign Defense. Nothing else.

Responding to abuse by the Dominican Republic

Full control of the border and commerce, with a response matched to the evidence. The ladder stops any future official from using it as a personal weapon. Try a case.

Haiti does not mirror the abuse

No raids on protected groups, no summary deportation, no detention of pregnant women or unaccompanied minors. Domestic immigration enforcement goes through the courts.

Receiving deportees

Reception at all four checkpoints: registration, medical triage, fast documentation, staffed by Section Agents working alongside existing civil-society groups.

Labour reciprocity

A Dominican firm employing Haitians must hold an audited contract on wages, standards and medical cover, enforced by the overseas legal-defence directorate (GLDD) and published.

Updated number The old text said about 670,500 deported since October 2024. That total wasn’t reconfirmed. Dominican migration authorities’ own counts give about 380,000 in 2025 and more than 226,500 from January to July 2026, which already tops 600,000.

Police

Starting point

About 13,500 officers for roughly 11.9 million people, with fewer than 300 of 570 sections covered (mid-2025 figures, not yet refreshed). The UN judges about 19,000 officers are needed. The goal is 19,000–20,000 over several years, building on the P4000 recruitment programme.

Retraining everyone

All serving officers complete procedural-justice training or leave. Bodycam footage is sampled and reviewed, not only checked after complaints. Better conduct earns better pay.

Georgia’s lesson, not its method

Georgia fired its entire traffic police in a day. The idea, a smaller, better-paid and vetted force, is sound. A mass firing while gangs hold the capital would create a vacuum. Haiti vets person by person.

Armed or not

Changed The draft’s “unarmed patrols” wasn’t realistic. On-duty officers carry an issued sidearm under a written use-of-force ladder. Weapons are locked up biometrically off duty.

Inside the force

Judicial Police feed the prosecution service. Administrative Police patrol. The Inspector-General’s findings now go to the DSI Oversight Commission as well, so the watchdog is watched. Officers rotate postings to prevent entanglement with local gangs.

Private security

Licensing, vetting and a firearms registry. Private firms can’t act as independent police.

Rollout follows the facts on the ground. A published, ledger-tracked map shows which communes have standing police and which don’t. No promise of everywhere, immediately.

The military: built with coup history in mind

  • Haiti disbanded the army in 1995 because of its coups and abuses, and re-created it in 2017. It has roughly 1,000–1,500 personnel, with a stated aim of 6,000 soon and 20,000 over four years.
  • No independent domestic policing. Roles: border defence, disaster response, and support to police. Each domestic deployment needs written civilian authorisation from Justice and Public Security, logged on the ledger. This clause is entrenched.
  • The Commander is proposed by a Defense Nominating Council (senior officers elected by peers + civilians chosen by the Assembly) and confirmed by supermajority. The removal path matches judges.
  • The only border-enforcement body is the Border Tactical Shield. Any internal deployment needs judicial emergency review and a sunset.

Embassies by tier

Today Haiti has roughly 19–27 embassies depending on the source, concentrated in the Americas. That count was not rechecked.

Tier 1

Full embassy

United States (plus its 6 consulates), Canada, Dominican Republic (plus 4 border consulates), France, Brazil, Chile, Bahamas.

Tier 2

New posts

India, South Korea, an African Union mission in Addis Ababa. Without these, the South-South strategy is only a paragraph.

Tier 3

Consulate or honorary consul

Smaller Caribbean and Latin American communities.

Staff are NASA Foreign Service graduates on a service bond. Every Tier 1 post is also a service point for the diaspora: passports, civil records, legal-aid intake, Brain-Gain Fund contact.

Haiti’s Asian recognition currently runs through Taiwan. The draft states this without taking a side. It carries real trade-offs in both directions.

Interference: what the design stops and what it can’t

Gets harder
  • Buying one person: no single bribable office, no campaign debts
  • Hacking one server: over 70% of copies must be altered
  • Capturing one court
  • Loan-conditionality pressure
  • Hiding payments: spending is public the day it happens
Untouched
  • Trade and remittance dependence
  • Disinformation aimed at voters
  • Arms trafficking
  • Cut cables and jammed uplinks
  • Diplomatic isolation after rejecting IMF terms

Respectful sovereignty

Courteous to every country, deferential to none. The structure, not one person’s nerve, prevents concessions.

South-South ties, in the open

The Bandung model: trade, culture, scholarships, through the UN, OAS, CARICOM and AU. No security dimension at all. Every agreement is public. The US stays a Tier 1 partner.

Emergency powers that end by default

0–48 hours

Council acts alone in an acute disaster or crisis.

72 hours

Constitutional Court reviews whether the State of Exception is proportionate.

2 weeks

Assembly ratifies by simple majority or it stops.

90 days

Ends automatically. Renewal needs two-thirds.

Even in an emergency: no cancelling the lottery draw (only a short, court-reviewed delay), no suspending court access, no closing the press. The reason: “the emergency required it” is the line history uses most.

Armed groups: judgment, surrender and a path out

The credible offer is not forgiveness. It is a fair hearing, a defined outcome and a way out. Three doors, each with a deadline.

Door 1 · children

Under 18 means victim. No prosecution for what the group made them do, transfer to child-protection centres, family tracing, school or a trade, and never an adult prison.

Door 2 · adults who surrender

Each person is seen by a Reintegration Panel: a judge, a prosecutor, a social worker, a victims’ advocate and a resident drawn by lot from the affected area.

Door 3 · the deadline passes

Anyone who keeps their weapons is arrested under normal law. Arrest comes first. Force follows published rules, body cameras and independent review of every death.

TrackWhoWhat happens
ACoerced or support roles, no grave crimesConditional release into the Reintegration Program, monitored.
BArmed robbery, extortion, assaultA reduced sentence in a skills prison, restitution, then supervised release.
CMurder, massacre, mass sexual violence, kidnapping leadership, trafficking, recruiting childrenNo amnesty. An ordinary trial. A reduced sentence only for full confession, weapons, evidence and naming financiers and suppliers.

Cut the money and the guns

Business people and officials who funded armed groups may come forward in a window for reduced sentences. After it closes, the ledger and the owner register are used to prosecute the rest, and their assets pay victims. Surrendered weapons are traced by serial number. There is no cash per gun: a surrendering area earns a road, school, clinic or water supply instead.

The program

A 12 to 24 month stipend paid into a bank account, never cash from a former patron. A certified trade. A community council consulted before anyone returns. For every person reintegrated, at least as many training places for young people who stayed out of the gangs. Protection from reprisal, and a separate protected track for women and girls.

Donors fund a trust chaired by Haitian authorities. Foreign staff and contractors do not run the operation or hold Haitian data, and the panel members and program staff follow the same gift rules as officials.

Skills prisons: jails that send people home with a trade

A skills prison cannot work if 82% of the people in it have never been convicted, so the first job is to empty the cells that should not be full.

First, clear the backlog

A legal maximum for pretrial detention, hearings held inside prisons, community service and supervised release for minor offences, and strict separation of untried from convicted, women from men and children from adults.

Small prisons near families

Regional prisons close to courts and relatives, with a separate high-security unit for gang leaders so they cannot run networks or recruit inside.

Real trades, real certificates

Literacy first, then construction, solar, electrical, welding, carpentry, plumbing, repair, farming, tailoring and digital skills. The same national exams as outside, and the certificate never mentions prison.

Fair wages, a way home

Prisoners are paid a fair rate. Part goes to family and victims, part to a savings account released on exit. A pre-release unit, a placement officer and a first-job guarantee follow.

Keep the prison from becoming a business. No private company may use prison labour, which would bring forced labour back through the side door. Workshops sell only to public bodies, such as school desks and hospital beds. Food and medicine are bought in open tenders with every meal’s cost published. Guards are rotated and covered by the gift rules. Inspections are unannounced.

A sovereign public force: no foreign command, the army on the Line

The Line (between crossing points)The Gates (the four official crossings)
WhoThe army, in a Border CommandCivilians: customs, the Border Tactical Shield, migration and health staff
JobSurveillance, patrols, stopping arms and armed groups, defence against attackInspection, tax, quarantine, identity, receiving returnees humanely
WhyThe 1987 Constitution gives the army the frontier, and only it can cover the whole lineMoney and people meet here. Giving soldiers control of customs would make the army a trading and taxing power

A person or load caught between gates is handed to the nearest civilian authority within 24 hours. The army does not investigate, charge or hold anyone longer. The ban on the army policing towns, markets, protests or elections stays entrenched.

A police that fits the population

Sized by a published coverage standard, with a staffed station in every communal section, about 30% women, recruits from every département, and posts away from the home commune with rotation. Every salary is tied to a biometric attendance record and each unit’s payroll is on the ledger, to catch ghost officers.

Ten rules against foreign command

  • Haitian command, always.
  • The UN-backed force is an ally, with published terms, not a commander.
  • No country supplies more than about a third of any training or equipment.
  • Haiti pays its own force. Salaries and promotions are never decided by a foreign party.
  • Foreign courses go by blind merit, and no foreign government pays an officer personally.
  • One logged channel for foreign intelligence. No direct access to Haitian databases.
  • Private security firms only in a declared emergency, under Haitian law.
  • Commanders hold no foreign citizenship or residence while in office.
  • No foreign base without two-thirds of the Assembly and a referendum.
  • The core rules are entrenched.

The Dominican Republic and the world: a strict gate, and a cost for disrespect

Strict means a customs service that cannot be bought

A computer picks which container is opened and by which inspector. Scanners at the four gates, one electronic declaration posted on the ledger, lot codes on imported food, licensed importers with their real owners listed, and a yearly report on market concentration. Inspectors rotate, and their lifestyle is audited. A small-trader fast lane keeps the market women in the legal economy.

Organic and safe, for everyone

A rule aimed only at Dominican goods would be discriminatory and would give the DR an easy case at the WTO. So the standard is the same for every origin and for Haitian producers: safety and labelling for all food imports, mandatory organic certification for named categories where supply exists, conformity certificates for non-food goods, and a voluntary organic label. Haiti builds its own organic capacity at the same time, and gets the same access for its goods in return. A published legal opinion comes first.

Never disrespect a Haitian: the cost of disrespect

The response is never violent, always lawful, always documented and always costly for whoever is responsible. A Haitian Rights Defense Service runs a 24-hour hotline in Kreyòl, consular visits, retained lawyers abroad, a case file for every incident and a diaspora-funded Defense Fund.

LevelMeasure
0Document the incident and support the victim, always.
1Diplomatic note, consular access, public report.
2Legal action: local courts, the Inter-American system, UN bodies, the ILO.
3Consequences for named people: entry bans, and evidence for countries with their own human-rights sanctions laws, so individuals rather than a whole nation face visa bans and asset measures.
4The trade ladder: tariff escalation or suspension of a category, with independent corroboration.
5Company-level pressure: a published Respect Index and complaints to buyers’ compliance units. Publish, do not boycott.

Guardrails. No single official can trigger anything above Level 2. Every restriction is published with the list of importers who gain and lose. Anyone with an interest in the category steps aside. Measures expire unless renewed with new evidence. This is where Israel’s example only partly transfers: Haiti has no military and alliance weight, so its leverage is evidence, law, the diaspora, diplomacy and trade.

Roots: the 1805 Charter and the 1987 Constitution

A system for Haiti should start from Haiti: the people who won their freedom, and the constitution in force today. This page says what to carry forward, what to leave behind, and which legal route is honest about the rules as written.

Document 0: the Dessalines Charter of 1805

It sits at the top of the public ledger, above every law, as a reminder of where Haitians come from. It is a symbol and a source of principles, not a model of government: it was an imperial text written under siege.

1805 textUsed today as
Art. 1: a state “sovereign and independent of any other power in the universe”Sovereignty as a working rule: no foreign command of the public force, no foreign money in campaigns, no secret concessions.
Art. 2: “Slavery is forever abolished”Entrenched, and extended to modern servitude: child domestic servitude (restavek), trafficking, debt bondage.
Art. 11: “Every citizen must possess a mechanic art”A National Skills Guarantee: every young Haitian leaves school or national service with a certified trade. It is the root of the skills prisons, the gang reintegration program and the technology hub.
Art. 12 (the principle): no foreigner as “master or proprietor”Limits on foreign land by residence and enterprise, never by race, plus a register of who really owns each company.
Art. 28: no leader may attach a private guard to his personNo personal guard corps for any official. Protection comes from a rotating public detail.
General Art. 4: the armed force is “essentially obedient”The public force obeys civil authority and never decides politics. Entrenched.
General Art. 5: no one is judged without being heardDue process for everyone, including members of armed groups who surrender.
Art. 36: the ruler shall never disturb neighboursThe no-violence doctrine: Haiti’s strength is law, evidence, diplomacy and trade.

How it lives in the system

  • It is the first entry on the public ledger.
  • Officials take an oath naming its principles, in front of their community.
  • A plaque and a public reading in every Sovereign House, and the first lesson of civic education.
  • The Preamble of any new constitution quotes its first articles.
  • It honours principles, not a person: the 1987 Constitution already bans the cult of personality.

What stays in 1805

  • All power in one office, and a ruler who judges his own cause.
  • A successor named by the ruler.
  • Loss of citizenship, even death, for those who emigrate: the diaspora is a pillar of this design.
  • Citizenship suspended by bankruptcy.
  • A racial legal category. Citizenship follows loyalty to the republic, not ancestry.
  • Military districts as the unit of government.

The legal route, told honestly

Article 284-3 forbids a referendum as a way to amend the 1987 Constitution. Article 284-2 delays any amendment until the next elected president is installed. Article 284-4 forbids changing the “democratic and republican nature” of the State. A Haitian constitutional lawyer must confirm this reading.

Track 1 · now

Ordinary law inside the 1987 text

Most of this design needs no constitutional change: the transition shield, orphanage rules, the gang program, skills prisons, the technology hub, police and army rules, and Dominican trade standards. The Dessalines Charter can be adopted as a ceremonial law.

Track 2 · pilot

Sortition as an advisory body

A lottery-selected panel advises Parliament and reviews the budget without taking any power from it. Nothing in the text prevents advice, and it builds trust first.

Track 3 · last

Constitutional change by the strict route

Arts. 282–284 are slow: realistically around 2031 for the decision and 2032 for its effect, on my reading. A replacement by a constituent assembly must be called what it is and rest on broad agreement.

Four serious efforts: a 2018 Chamber committee, the 2021 presidential draft, the 2024–25 working group, and the May 2025 steering committee draft. Each was asked the same questions: who gains power, who paid for the drafting, is there a single point of capture, was the process lawful, does it make some Haitians lesser, does it create outside dependency.

How to read any reform proposal, including this one

  1. Judge the text, the money and the process, never the author’s ancestry or colour. Colour and class suspicion is itself a form of capture. The structural answer is anonymous exams, lotteries and published statistics.
  2. Ask who funded the drafting, and publish the answer.
  3. Ask whether it creates a single point of capture.
  4. Ask whether the process is lawful and open.
  5. Ask whether any Haitian becomes legally lesser than another.
  6. Ask whether it makes Haiti depend on an outside actor for money, force or legitimacy.

The shield: from the draw to the swearing-in

The easiest way to capture an official is not to bribe them in office. It is to own them before they hold office. Right after someone is elected or drawn, private interests say “you are an official now, you cannot keep living in that house” and supply a hotel suite, a driver, an office at a luxury hotel, cash, women and rice bags for the home commune. By the day the official is sworn in, they already owe. The rule: the state fills that vacuum before a private party can.

The danger is larger in a lottery system, because drawn members are ordinary citizens who may be poor and suddenly exposed, and because attackers will concentrate on the gap after the draw.

The retreat teaches the capture playbook out loud: the hotel, the car, the cash, the women, the rice. Members hear it from people who have seen it, before it happens to them, and meet the Integrity Desk in person so the reporting channel is a face, not a form.

The rules from Day 0

Zero-gift rule

No gift, loan, lodging, transport, job, sex or donation in their name from anyone with an interest in a state decision. Ordinary hospitality has a published ceiling.

The offer is reportable

Every offer goes to the Integrity Desk within 24 hours and into a public Offer Register.

A secret has no power once disclosed

Report within 72 hours and you are treated as a victim. The organisers are prosecuted. After the window, silence is presumed corruption.

Contact in daylight

Meetings with business representatives happen in a public room at the Sovereign House, with two note-takers and a public log. A Public Consultation Register gives them a proper channel.

Disclosure on Day 0

Assets, accounts, foreign residence, company interests and family business ties, published on the ledger and checked against outside records.

Hotels and car rentals must report

Any booking or rental for an official paid by a third party is reported within 48 hours, or the business risks its licence.

Hitting the giver, and ending the deferred bribe

The giver
  • Offering a prohibited benefit is a crime, accepted or not.
  • The firm and its real owners are barred from public contracts, licences and import permits for 10 years.
  • A law that passed with the vote of a member who took an undisclosed benefit can be voided in court, and the gains are given back. The buyer cannot keep what he bought.
  • Executives are personally liable, and the case is published.
The exit
  • 12 months of bridge pay and the right to return to a previous job or a comparable placement.
  • A cooling-off period: 2 years for Assembly members, 3 years for the Council, ministers and regulators.
  • Protection for those who vote against powerful interests continues after the term.
How it is found
  • The official’s own protected report.
  • Payment signals on the national payment rail.
  • Whistleblowers, including hotel staff and drivers.
  • Random, court-authorised integrity tests, with the Integrity Desk’s own staff tested too.
  • A “who benefits” report 14 days before votes on customs, concessions, mining, energy or telecom.

It can start now. For the 2026–27 elections, ordinary law can already bring in a gift ban from the day results are proclaimed, disclosure moved to that day, hotel and car-rental reporting, and company debarment. State housing and a vehicle pool cost little compared with one captured law.

Is it bullet-proof? No, and here is the test

No design is. The test for every office, rule and flow of money: if this were captured, what stops it, and is that a different body, chosen differently, on a different timeline?

Controls the review added

  • A public register of the real owners of every company that wins public business, and of large rural land.
  • Concessions: open tender, full contract published within 7 days, two-thirds Assembly vote, a binding audit-court review, a sunset, no secret arbitration clause.
  • A Competition Authority against cartels in imports and distribution.
  • Foreign government money to organisations is disclosed, never punished, and the rule is reviewed every 3 years.
  • Rotation, lifestyle audits and written reasons for the permanent officials where corruption moves.
What no design can fix
  • A judiciary that can be bought or frightened.
  • Physical threats against officials and witnesses.
  • The absence of a cost model.
  • The slow legal route.
  • Dependence on remittances, food and fuel imports.
  • Disinformation: the state may answer it, never censor it.

Next step A published adversarial review by Haitian jurists, auditors, police and prison officers, farmers, merchants and diaspora specialists, with anti-corruption practitioners paid by Haiti, and an open call for anyone to try to break the design.

  • Public results show totals only. A group of fewer than 20 people is not shown.
